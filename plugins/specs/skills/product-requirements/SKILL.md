# Product requirements document (PRD)

## What is PRD?

A PRD states the problem, who has it, and what must be true for it to be solved — in enough detail that a team can agree they are building the right thing, and later tell whether they did.

## The contents

What a PRD covers.

- **Problem/Opportunity** — what is wrong today, what issues are caused when that feature is missing, why this is a valuable opportunity for the team to pursue.
- **Users & use cases** — who hits the problem, and the situation they are in when they do.
- **Goals & success metrics** — the intended change in the world, each paired with how you would know it happened.
- **Non-goals** — what is deliberately excluded.
- **Requirements** — what functional and non-functional requirements of the product needs to be implemented for the goals to be met. Can be written around user journeys, plain statements, acceptance criteria, or rules, depending on what carries the requirement.
- **Constraints & dependencies** — what limits the solution space.
- **Open questions** — what is still undecided, and who decides it.

## Main principles

- **A PRD is written at the level of the user's experience**: A PRD describes what must become true for users, not how the system will make it true. It is not a design doc — how the system will do it belongs elsewhere.
